Training a model, fine-tuning one, or running a large analysis takes GPUs. As more courses teach machine learning, more students need them, and the campus platform has not always had enough to go around. Some students have paid for cloud GPU time out of their own pockets to finish coursework and projects.

PARASOL is the campus response. It is the platform students already know, formerly called DSMLP, with more GPUs, a reservation system that matches capacity to the academic calendar, and a name that reflects who it serves.

## What the name stands for

PARASOL is short for **Platform for Applied Research, Analytics, Science and Open Learning**. The platform supports classroom GPU demand first, starting with prioritized access for Halıcıoğlu School of Data Science and Computing students and faculty.

## What was added in fall 2026

Enabled by Taner Halıcıoğlu's founding gift, the Halıcıoğlu School added four GPU servers to the platform:

- 32 NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs in total
- Nearly four times the platform's previous peak capacity for instruction
- About 66,800 peak GPU-hours a year for coursework and student projects
- More than 213,000 off-peak GPU-hours a year for approved research, capstones, and graduate work

[GPU Hardware and CUDA](/docs/gpu-access/gpu-hardware.html) lists every GPU model on the cluster, including the new ones, and the GPU class each one serves.

## Why reservations

GPU demand is uneven. It peaks around assignment deadlines and in tenth week and finals, and it falls overnight and on weekends. A reservation holds a GPU for a specific window so a student with a deadline is not stuck behind a walk-up queue. Off-peak hours cost fewer Service Units, which moves long research runs to the hours when classes need the least.

[Reservations](/docs/gpu-access/reservations.html) explains how to book a window. [Service Units and Budgets](/docs/gpu-access/service-units-and-budgets.html) explains how GPU time is metered.

## What comes next

The new servers also give the campus better data on GPU demand. University leaders will watch how the capacity is used to plan further investment. That includes software and services that make GPU computing easier to use in courses outside science and engineering.

## Experience that carries over

PARASOL runs containers on Kubernetes, with the same tools and workflows used in industry and research computing. Students in undergraduate, master's, and doctoral programs learn on infrastructure like the one they will meet after graduation.
